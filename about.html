Why this exists

Small businesses do not need another platform. They need one file that works.

Most of the businesses I talk to already own the right tool. Excel is installed, the staff know it, and the data is already there. What is missing is structure: a data model that holds up, formulas that cannot quietly break, and reporting that builds itself.

So that is what I build. Not a template downloaded from somewhere — a system designed around the way your business actually operates, including the exceptions nobody documents.

My background is software: Python, logic, interactive tools. That background is why the workbooks I ship avoid the usual Excel traps — copy-pasted formulas, hard-coded months, five versions of the same sheet, and numbers nobody can trace back to a source.

Principles

One source of truth

Every number on a dashboard traces back to one row in one table. No typed summaries, no parallel totals.

Make it hard to break

Validated inputs, protected formulas and conditional formatting that shows a mistake before it becomes one.

Document everything

A short manual and a readable structure, so the file survives staff changes — not just my memory of it.

Honest about limits

If Excel is the wrong tool, you will hear it in the first call. A workbook is not an ERP, and I will not pretend it is.

Working with me

Direct, not departmental.

You message one person. You get one voice, one blueprint, one invoice. That is the whole advantage of a studio this small: nothing gets lost in a handover, and nobody builds a feature you did not ask for.
